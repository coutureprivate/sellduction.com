2. What are cookies and similar technologies?

A cookie is a small text file that a website places on your computer, phone or tablet when you visit it. Cookies allow a website to recognize your device, remember your preferences and understand how the site is used. Cookies set by the website you are visiting are called "first-party cookies"; cookies set by other services through that website are "third-party cookies". Cookies may be "session" cookies, deleted when you close your browser, or "persistent" cookies, which stay on your device until they expire or you delete them.

Similar technologies include browser local storage, pixels, web beacons and scripts. For simplicity, this policy refers to all of them as "cookies".

3. Cookies and technologies used on this Website

3.1 Strictly necessary No consent required

These technologies are essential for the Website to work and to remember the choice you make in the cookie banner. They do not track you across other sites.

  • sdCookieConsent (browser local storage, set by the Website) - stores whether you clicked "Accept" or "Decline" in the cookie banner, so that the banner is not shown on every visit. It remains until you clear your browser's site data.
  • Hosting and security - our hosting provider (Vercel) may use strictly necessary technical cookies or headers to deliver pages securely and protect against abuse.

Our hosting provider may also produce privacy-friendly, aggregated traffic statistics based on server requests, without setting cookies.

4. Your consent and how to change it

On your first visit, the Website shows a cookie banner. By clicking "Accept" you consent to the use of the non-essential cookies described in this policy. By clicking "Decline" only strictly necessary technologies are used; third-party content such as videos and forms may then work with limited functionality. Browsing the Website without making a choice does not activate non-essential analytics cookies of our own.

You can change your choice at any time by clearing the Website's site data (cookies and local storage) in your browser settings; the banner will then appear again on your next visit and you can make a new choice. Withdrawing consent does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal.

Where you are located in the EU/EEA or the United Kingdom, consent is requested in line with the ePrivacy rules and the GDPR / UK GDPR. If you are in a jurisdiction that applies an opt-out model (for example certain US states), you may decline cookies through the banner and your browser settings as described below.

5. How to manage cookies in your browser

All modern browsers let you view, delete and block cookies. You can usually:

  • Delete cookies - remove all or selected cookies already stored on your device.
  • Block cookies - configure the browser to refuse some or all cookies, or third-party cookies only.
  • Get notified - ask the browser to warn you each time a cookie is about to be set.
  • Use private / incognito mode - cookies are deleted when you close the window.

You will find the relevant settings under "Privacy and security" in Google Chrome, "Privacy" in Safari (Preferences on Mac, Settings on iPhone and iPad), "Privacy & Security" in Mozilla Firefox and "Cookies and site permissions" in Microsoft Edge. Each browser's help pages explain the exact steps for your version. You can also opt out of Google's personalization through your Google account settings. Please note that blocking all cookies may prevent parts of this Website, in particular embedded videos and sign-up forms, from working properly.
